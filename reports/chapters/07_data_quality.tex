\section{What each system uniquely catches}

dbt \file{schema.yml} tests run with each build: unique plus not-null keys, nullable-aware relationships (a \file{where} clause exempts legitimate null FKs), accepted ranges on amounts and the \file{line_amount} expression. The standalone SQL suite (\file{src/jobs/sql_test.py} over \file{tests/data-quality/}) does what dbt cannot express generically: schema-wide duplicate, null, format, negative, range and spacing sweeps, hardcoded domain allows (countries, payment methods, order statuses), six business rules including \file{order total = SUM(lines)}, and the silver-to-gold row-count parity check, which is the only test proving promotion completeness. GX (\cmd{python -m gx.run --layer all}) validates value distributions without dbt, adds \file{silver_loaded_at}/\file{gold_loaded_at} presence as a load-job signal, and exits 1 on data failure versus 2 on build error.

Known tension, stated honestly: GX asserts FK ids \file{NotNull} while dbt relationships deliberately permit nulls. If unknown-brand products are legitimate, the GX suites need relaxing, not the models. Conventions: a \file{SELECT} returning rows fails, a \file{DO} block raising fails, and GX runs every requested layer unless \cmd{--fail-fast} is passed.

\begin{keybox}
\item Three systems, three blind spots covered: declarative FKs, business aggregates with parity, runner-independent values.
\item Counts in \cref{tab:dq-counts} are regenerated from the repo; rerun the collector after model changes.
\item The GX-versus-dbt null-FK tension is open and documented, not silently resolved.
\end{keybox}
